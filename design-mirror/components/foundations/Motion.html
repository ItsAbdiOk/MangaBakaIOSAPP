Every one of these degrades to an instant state change under prefers-reduced-motion (mirrored here too — try your OS setting). In the app this is Motion.isReduced, read from UIAccessibility rather than the SwiftUI environment so models and closures can honour it too.

Hover the chip

snappy — 220ms, quick ease-out

SwiftUI: response 0.28, damping 0.86 (slightly underdamped). Taps and toggles — answers a finger rather than settling into place. Used by Eyebrow's counting label and LibraryControl's state swap.

Hover the chip

settle — 320ms, damped ease

SwiftUI: response 0.45, damping 0.82. Content arriving, sheets presenting — a touch slower than snappy so a whole surface doesn't feel jumpy. Backs ArrivesModifier (rise 8pt + unblur + fade) and AppearsSoftlyModifier (image over its BlurHash).

Hover the chip

glide — 420ms, fully damped

SwiftUI: response 0.7, damping 1.0 (no overshoot). Scroll-linked motion — EnterScaleModifier's card scale-up and ParallaxModifier's depth drift, so nothing bounces while a finger is still moving the content.

Hover the row

stagger — 45ms/item, capped at 6

Motion.stagger(index): each item's settle delayed by its place in the group, so a list assembles rather than snapping in at once. Capped at 6 steps so a long list's last rows don't wait seconds. Feeds ArrivesModifier(index:) via Motion.arrival(index:).
