## H4. On-device memory — ANSWERED, with one honest limit

**The question.** The 99.3 MB peak in the review is a simulator number.
`xctrace --template Allocations --launch` had recorded no allocation tables,
and the reason was unknown.

**Why Allocations records nothing on a device.** Reproduced twice tonight on
the real iPhone 16 Pro (`00008140-0005191901A2801C`), Release build, once
plain and once with `--env MallocStackLogging=1 --env
MallocStackLoggingNoCompact=1`. Both runs completed and both emitted:

    Timeline modification failed -- dylibs overlap by 0x148000:
        0x103ea8000-0x103ff0000 [0x148000] (0-3562453885453) /usr/lib/dyld
        0x103ea8000-0x103ff0000 [0x148000] (0-9223372036854775807) /usr/lib/dyld

The exported table of contents lists the Allocations *track* and its
recording settings, but the `<data>` section contains no allocation schema at
all — only `kdebug`, `os-signpost`, `thread-info`, `tick` and friends. The
instrument attached and captured nothing. Setting the malloc environment
variables changed nothing: they appear in the trace's own settings dump and
the data tables are byte-for-byte the same set.

**Negative result:** on-device Allocations is not reachable through `xctrace`
in this Xcode (16.0 / 17F113). It needs Instruments' own launch configuration.
Not worth more time — the number it would give is obtainable another way.

**The number, from Activity Monitor instead.** Same device, Release build,
45 seconds from launch, 17 samples:

| measure | peak | median | settled |
|---|---|---|---|
| physical footprint | **44.8 MB** | 42.9 MB | 42.8 MB |
| resident size | 141.4 MB | 140.9 MB | 140.8 MB |
| anonymous | 44.5 MB | 42.6 MB | 42.5 MB |

Physical footprint is the one that matters: it is what iOS's jetsam limits
are measured against. Resident size counts shared framework pages that every
app on the phone shares, so 141 MB is not 141 MB of ours.

**Verdict: nothing to fix.** 45 MB on launch is unremarkable for an app of
this kind, and nowhere near memory pressure.

**The limit, stated plainly.** The device was not driven during the trace —
nobody was awake to touch it, and there is no touch injection on a real
device without the QA bridge that was declined. So this is launch-and-idle on
the device, and the simulator's 99.3 MB was an interactive peak after
scrolling a 939-series library. They are not the same workload and should not
be compared as though they were. What is now known is the floor on real
hardware, not the ceiling.

## H2. Why series titles fail contrast — ANSWERED, and it shrinks C1

**The question.** Apple's audit reports "Contrast failed" on "ONE PIECE",
"Solo Leveling", "RUNAWAY FAMILY" and other titles drawn in `textPrimary`,
which measures 18.37:1 against the app's ground. The report could not be
about the colour, so it had to be about what is really behind the text — and
without coordinates that could only be guessed at.

**How it was reproduced.** The audit now records each issue's frame, and
saves the screen exactly as it saw it (`/tmp/mb-a11y-<screen>.png`). Both
were needed: the audit launches its own app, so a screenshot taken afterwards
by hand shows a different feed at a different scroll position. That was tried
first and it answered a question nobody had asked — the frames landed on the
wrong content and a title that genuinely passes looked like it failed.

With the real pair, every failing element's own pixels can be sampled.

**Three causes, and only one of them is ours.**

**1. Most of them are off-screen.** "Hidden gems" is at y=861 on an 852pt
screen — entirely below the bottom edge. "Breaking A Romantic Fantasy
Villain" is at y=847, so 5 of its 18 points are visible. Sampling their
frames returns uniform black or uniform ground: there is no text there to
measure. The audit walks the accessibility hierarchy, which includes rows a
lazy stack has built but not shown, and reports a contrast failure for each
one. **Audit artefact, not a defect.** This accounts for the majority of the
47 contrast issues, which means C1's headline number was never 47.

**2. Text passing under the floating tab bar — real, and small.** "Solo
Leveling" samples a background of (62,62,67) rather than the ground's
(8,8,11): it is behind the tab bar's glass. The title itself still clears
(9.69), but the meta line beneath it does not — "Manhwa · 8.6" measures 3.23
against what is actually behind it. Content scrolling under the translucent
bar is intended, and `scrollBottomInset` guarantees everything can be scrolled
clear of it, but while it is under there the smallest, dimmest line in the app
is the least legible thing on screen.

**3. SKIP and SAVE over live cover artwork — checked, and withdrawn.** The
badges on a stack card sample backgrounds of (104,221,255) and (57,184,254):
bright cyan, which is the cover art behind them, at 3.09:1. That looked like
the one genuinely bad case in the set and it was written up as such. It is
not. `badgeStrength` is `abs(drag.width) / divisor`, and the card is not being
dragged in a static audit, so both badges are at `opacity(0)`: the sampler was
measuring bare artwork with no badge on it. Cause 1 again, in a different
disguise.

Worth doing the arithmetic for the case that does happen, since the whole
point of the badge is that it appears over an unpredictable image. Mid-drag,
`surfaceBadge` (#0A0A0C at 0.60) over the worst background in that screenshot
composites to (48,94,109); `textPrimary` over that gives 5.8:1. It clears AA.
Nothing to fix.

**What this changes.** C1 was written as "47 contrast issues, `textQuaternary`
at 2.52:1 is the cause, and fixing it means a decision about collapsing the
type ramp". The measurement does not support that. The palette is mostly
fine; the failures cluster on text drawn over something, and the fix is to
give those few places a background rather than to change the colours
everywhere.

**Process note.** The commit that fixed this was made with a broken build:
`swiftlint --strict` was clean, the test command was run, and its compile
errors were four lines above the grep that only looked for the test summary.
Amended within the minute. The gate has to read the build result, not just
the part of the output that was asked for.

## C2 (not an unknown, but resolved the same way) — "Text clipped" is not a defect

Two entries survive on Discover. Both were checked against the audit's own
screenshot:

- **"Gambling Apocalypse Kaiji"** renders in full, on two lines, inside its
  106x33 frame. Nothing is clipped. The audit compares the text's intrinsic
  single-line width against the frame, which a `lineLimit(2)` title will
  always lose. Artefact.
- **"Let's Buy the Land and Cultivate It in a Different World"** truncates to
  "Let's Buy the Land and Cultiv…". It has an ellipsis, it is a 56-character
  title in a 112pt card, and VoiceOver is given the whole thing. A grid stays
  a grid because its cards are the same size; the App Store's own does this.
  By design.

No change. Recorded so the two lines in the audit report are not mistaken for
work outstanding.

## Found while doing H1: a whole screen is unreachable — NEEDS ABDI

`ShelfDetailView` exists, has its own inline search, is covered by tests, and
`RootView` carries a navigation destination for it. Nothing opens it.

The two views that used to — `LibraryView.shelfCards` and
`LibraryView.searchResults`, both of which built `ShelfCard`s and called
`onOpenShelf` — had no callers at all and were removed on 2026-09-11 as dead
code. They were already unreferenced before tonight; Periphery is what
surfaced them. `onOpenShelf` is still passed into `LibraryView` from
`RootView` and is never invoked.

What the Library shows instead is a filter row, the shape bar and a flat
`LibraryList`. Tapping "Reading 71" filters the list in place. That is a
reasonable replacement for shelves-as-cards, and it may well be the deliberate
newer design — the accompanying comment says the board did not draw the
schedule and taste cards either.

**Two ways to resolve it, and it is a product decision rather than a bug fix:**

1. **Delete it.** `ShelfDetailView`, `ShelfCard`, the `openShelf` state and the
   `onOpenShelf` plumbing all go. The filter row is the shelf experience.
   Honest, and about 300 lines lighter.
2. **Wire it back up.** The screen does things the filtered list does not — a
   search scoped to one shelf, its own ordering. Putting the cards back means
   deciding where they sit relative to the filter row, which is a design
   question.

Nothing was changed either way. Working code is left in place because that is
the reversible choice; the audit test for the screen skips with this reason
printed, so the question stays visible in every run until it is answered.

## H6. Does the tab bar restore on scroll-up? — ANSWERED: yes

The device review recorded this as unverified: the bar minimised on scroll
down as `.tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown)` intends, and did not visibly
come back under mouse-wheel scrolling through iPhone Mirroring. The note said
it "needs a finger to confirm".

Confirmed on the simulator, which injects real touches:

1. Swipe up over 450pt in 0.25s — the bar minimises to the current tab's icon
   plus the search circle. Correct.
2. Swipe down over 400pt in 0.25s — **still minimised**. Twice.
3. Swipe down over 530pt in 0.1s — the full capsule comes back: Discover,
   Stack, Mix, Library, and the search circle.

So it restores, and the review's guess that the mirror was the problem was
right — but only by accident. The real threshold is the gesture, not the
input device: a slow drag upward does not bring it back, a flick does. That
is iOS's own behaviour for `.onScrollDown` and not something this app sets.

**Nothing to fix.** Recorded because "it did not come back" is what two
separate sessions saw before pushing hard enough.

## H1. The six unaudited screens — CLOSED, five of six

Not audited by hand once, but added to the accessibility audit so they are
measured on every run: the blocked-tags screen, the cover gallery, the
Library with search results showing, and a stack card held mid-drag. With the
four tab roots, the series page and Settings, that is ten screens.

Shelf detail is the sixth and cannot be audited, because nothing in the app
opens it. See the section above.

**What adding them found, which was not the point of adding them:** the
existing series-detail audit had never been on the series page. It tapped
`app.scrollViews.buttons.firstMatch` on Discover, which is the "Open the
stack" card, so it had been auditing the Stack and filing the results under
"Series detail". Every screen now proves it is itself before anything is
measured, and failing to arrive fails the test.

## D2 — `ReleaseSchedule.swift` at 10.9%: partly answered, partly left

The finding read "the cadence estimator that prints '38 days overdue' is 10.6%
covered across 312 lines". Two things turned out to be true and neither was
what the number suggested.

**The estimator is not in that file and is well covered.** `Cadence.estimate`
lives in `Cadence.swift` and has its own suite, including the regularity
spread and the overdue states.

**The scope rules are not in that file's coverage either, but they are
tested** — in `CharacterTests.swift`, of all places, under a suite called
"Only unfinished series get a release estimate". A duplicate of them was
written tonight before that was found, and deleted. Worth knowing where they
live: a grep for `ReleaseScheduleService` finds them, a look at the file list
does not.

**What is genuinely untested** is the actor's own work: `worksInScope`,
`snapshot`, the build loop, and the GRDB cache read/write. All of it needs a
database and a fake MangaUpdates client, which is a fixture worth building and
not one to rush at 3am alongside the rest of tonight's list. Left with the
reason rather than half-done.
